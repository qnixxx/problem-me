ROOM 01 / 04

THE SHORTEST LINE IS NOT THE FASTEST LINE

A queue can look short while containing tasks with very different durations. A complicated return, payment problem, or chat at the till can outweigh several quick purchases.

The Bureau has installed three motionless checkout lines, each accompanied by a different kind of impatience. The middle queue appears fastest only from a distance.

This is a fine example of confusing a visible measure—number of people—with the outcome we actually care about: waiting time.

“IT'S ALWAYS THE OTHER QUEUE” IS A FEELING, NOT YET A DATASET.

ROOM 02 / 04

THE QUANTUM QUEUE TRANSFER ENGINE

Professor Pebble proposes mounting every shopping cart on tiny rails so customers can be moved between queues by a central computer.

The engine calculates ideal position with impressive precision. Unfortunately, the rail system costs more than the supermarket and briefly relocates the dairy aisle into a neighboring village.

The prototype is discontinued after it invents a new kind of traffic jam entirely inside the checkout area.

RESULT / WAIT TIME REDUCED BY ZERO MINUTES. DRAMA INCREASED CONSIDERABLY.

ROOM 03 / 04

THE PROVINCE OF WAITING

This wide landscape includes train delays, elevator stops, loading bars at 99%, and the toast that finishes only after you check on it.

Not all delays are alike. Some are unavoidable, some poorly communicated, and some painful mainly because we keep comparing our experience to an imaginary faster alternative.

The Bureau's atlas marks the Valley of Regret near the border of the Kingdom of Hindsight.

NEARBY WEATHER / SCATTERED “I SHOULD HAVE PICKED THAT ONE”.

> REFLECTION / 003-D

☼
ROOM 04 / 04

THE COST OF COMPARING

A slower queue is frustrating, but watching another line move faster can make the same wait feel worse. We become occupied not only with waiting, but with whether we chose correctly.

Sometimes there is no reliable signal available at the moment of choice. A sensible decision can still produce an unlucky outcome. That doesn't retroactively make it a bad decision.

Perhaps the most useful skill is learning when to stop comparing and simply let a decent choice stand.

A SLOWER QUEUE IS STILL MOVING.
