THE MELON CART THAT WAS ALWAYS LATE_

A delivery is late. The cart is innocent.

01 / INCIDENT REPORT

The late arrival

The picnic was planned for 10:05. Inspector Mango and the melon cart arrived at 10:42. Everybody blamed the tiny cart wheels. But in the Bureau's fictional dispatch log, the cart left at 09:37 rather than the planned 09:00; the journey itself took its usual five minutes.

What looked like slow transport was mostly a late departure. That is the first fact the crew can check against the record.

OBSERVED: delayed departure, ordinary travel time.

02 / FOLLOW FIVE WHYS

A chain of testable explanations

Why was the delivery late? Because departure was 37 minutes behind schedule. Why the late departure? Because the crew needed a replacement melon. Why a replacement? The morning list said twelve, but the shelf held eleven. Why the mismatch? A melon used for an evening tasting was never recorded on the checklist. Why was the update missed? The closing routine had no assigned inventory handoff and relied on somebody remembering.

Each why should point to an observable condition, not a guess about who's careless. Five is a guide to deeper inquiry, not a mandatory stopping point.

NEXT TEST: inspect several closing checklists and match them to morning shelf counts.

03 / OBSERVATION VS HYPOTHESIS

Check the final link

The dispatch time, shelf count and old checklist support the beginning of the chain. The lack of a named handoff is a candidate explanation for why the mismatch recurred, but a single morning doesn't prove it. Check supplier records too: an unrecorded shortage might give a different explanation.

A stronger test compares several shifts, with and without a closing reconciliation, and observes whether count mismatches and departures improve.

ALTERNATIVE: supplier shortage; distinguish that with delivery receipts.

04 / WHAT CHANGES

Fix the routine, not the cart

The Bureau adds a two-minute closing count, a shared list update and a named handoff to the morning crew. It tracks dispatches for a week rather than celebrating one punctual trip.

5 Whys turns a complaint about a person or object into a candidate system improvement that can be verified.

TAKEAWAY: a useful root cause is both evidenced and changeable.
