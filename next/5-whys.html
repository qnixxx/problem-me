CASE #001 // THE MISSING SNACKS

5 WhysFollow the cause.

A simple technique for moving past the first explanation. Ask “why?” repeatedly until the symptom gives way to a cause worth acting on.

PURPOSEROOT CAUSE
DIFFICULTYEASY
TIME15–30 MIN
BEST FORCAUSE CHAINS
STATUS: CONCERNED
PROBLEM_

Capybara's afternoon watermelon is missing.

The empty bowl is the symptom. We could blame the obvious suspect — or investigate.

↓ WHY?
🥣  ❌  🍉
CAPYBARA.EXE / INSPECTING SCENE
WHY #1

Why is there no watermelon?

Because somebody ate it.

↓ WHY?
🐦  →  🍉
STATUS: SUSPECT LOCATED
WHY #2

Why was it eaten?

A bird got into the snack area and helped itself.

↓ WHY?
🐦 ↘  🚪  🔓
STATUS: NOTING SECURITY FLAW
WHY #3

Why could the bird get in?

The snack-area gate had been left open.

↓ WHY?
🧑  →  🚪  💭❓
STATUS: MEMORY IS NOT A CONTROL
WHY #4

Why was the gate left open?

Closing it depended on someone remembering after every visit.

↓ WHY?
🚪  ＋  ⚙️❌
ROOT CAUSE SIGNAL DETECTED
WHY #5

Why does it depend on memory?

There is no self-closing mechanism or other control in the process.

ROOT CAUSE FOUND_

The process relies on memory.

“The bird ate it” explains what happened. A gate that can repeatedly be left open explains why the problem can keep happening.

🚪 ⚙️ ✅   ☕
STATUS: VINDICATED
COUNTERMEASURE_

Make the gate self-closing.

Now the fix targets the repeatable system weakness rather than merely chasing birds.

REAL WORLD TRANSLATION_

Missing watermelon→Your incident
Bird→Immediate cause
Open gate→Process failure
No auto-close→Systemic cause

REMEMBER_

Five is not magic. Ask enough whys to reach something useful and evidence-based. Stop if the chain becomes speculation, branches into multiple causes, or you reach something outside the problem's scope.
